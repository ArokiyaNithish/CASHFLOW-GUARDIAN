# MSME Payment Norms

Under the MSMED Act of 2006, buyers must pay MSME suppliers within 45 days of acceptance of goods or services, if there is a written agreement. In the absence of an agreement, the payment must be made within 15 days.
If the buyer fails to make the payment within this period, they are liable to pay compound interest to the MSME supplier. The interest rate is three times the bank rate notified by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI). This interest is compounded with monthly rests.

Key implications for buyers:
- Ensure timely payments to avoid hefty interest penalties.
- Accurate tracking of invoice dates and MSME registration status of suppliers is crucial.
- The interest paid for delayed payments to MSMEs is not allowed as a deductible expense under the Income Tax Act, increasing the financial burden on the buyer.

For MSME suppliers:
- Registering under Udyam ensures legal backing to demand timely payments.
- Suppliers can approach the Micro and Small Enterprise Facilitation Council (MSEFC) for dispute resolution and recovery of dues.
- Maintaining proper documentation (invoices, delivery challans, agreements) is essential to claim delayed payments and interest.

This act ensures working capital stability for MSMEs by discouraging delayed payments from large corporate buyers.
